# KORLIX Social

## Discover: news and community videos

Apply `20261004031046_korlix_social_discover.sql`, deploy the backend, then deploy Flutter web. Open **Social → Discover · News & videos**. The mixed feed has News, Videos and Saved views; news topics include Jamaica & Caribbean, World, Business, Technology, Sports and Entertainment. Videos have Latest, Following and My videos views. News uses original AI-written summaries and exact retrieved HTTPS source links, with publication and checked dates. Publisher photography and full articles are not republished. It is a shared recent-news edition, not personalized ranking, an exhaustive feed or a breaking-news guarantee. `OPENAI_API_KEY` enables research using the existing chat model, low reasoning effort, at most four web searches per edition. A database lease limits refresh to once every three hours across instances, with a 15-minute failure backoff. Startup warms the cache once; subsequent refreshes are demand-driven. No Social member content or preferences are sent for news research. Failed or unsupported research never invents a substitute edition.

Videos accept MP4/MOV/WebM, 0.5–60 seconds, at most 50 MiB and 9 megapixels. ffprobe validates containers and duration; ffmpeg produces metadata-stripped H.264/AAC MP4 and a JPEG thumbnail. Container/protocol allowlists reject playlists; processing uses two threads, bounded time and at most two concurrent uploads per instance. Limits are ten new uploads/day, fifty retained videos, and 500 MiB of media reservations/storage per member. Immutable upload IDs/checksums make transport retries safe. Uploading creates a private draft; publication requires explicit rights/community-rules confirmation. Unpublished drafts expire after 24 hours. No AI model receives user videos through this workflow.

Video cards have likes and saves; both types can be saved and reported. Source links can be copied for sharing. Publishing is visible to eligible signed-in Social members. Feeds and fresh playback links honor both directions of blocking and suspension; Following uses accepted connections. The full-screen viewer supports vertical swipes, tap-to-play, pause and scrubbing, and disposes playback on clip/account changes. Previewing, leaving the app and route coverage stop or pause media. Viewer pagination uses the loaded feed batch; load more clips in the feed before opening them. This first release does not include comments on videos, creator monetization, personalized ranking or K-Nova narration.

The video bucket, metadata, marks and RPCs are service-only with RLS and a restrictive browser-deny storage policy. Thumbnail URLs expire after 60 seconds and video links after 90 seconds; buffered/downloaded media cannot be recalled. Reports enter the existing moderator queue with explicit reported-video playback and remove/resolve actions. Content is not represented as pre-reviewed by a human. Removing a video hides it immediately. Durable garbage collection survives profile deletion and races with in-flight uploads, waits at least ten minutes, and retries every thirty minutes. Account deletion cascades video/mark metadata and queues media deletion. Saved news is retained until unsaved/deleted; unsaved cache items older than thirty days are pruned. Minimal report evidence can remain for moderation.

Validation: `node --test backend/test/social_discover.test.mjs backend/test/social.test.mjs backend/test/social_communication.test.mjs backend/test/social_wall_voice.test.mjs` exercises actual SQL/RLS, ownership, keyset pagination, blocks, saves, reporting/removal, leases, retry/cleanup and real ffmpeg output. Flutter `test/social_discover_test.dart` covers responsive feeds, actions, explicit publish consent, playback lifetime and account changes. Automated video I/O uses a platform fixture; physical Safari/Android/iOS playback remains a device acceptance check.

Native Flutter entry: **For personal use → KORLIX Social**. This release adds opt-in member profiles, approval-based connections, private text conversations, and category forums. It uses the existing KORLIX account, backend, and Supabase project; there are no new paid services or AI calls.

## Permissions and behavior

- `requireUser` verifies the access token with Supabase Auth. Anonymous and unconfirmed accounts cannot use Social. Actor identity always comes from that verified user, never a body or query parameter.
- All Social tables have RLS and explicitly deny browser roles. The security-invoker RPC and helpers are executable only by `service_role`. Deploy the migration before the backend.
- Auth user IDs/emails are not returned in member cards. Social uses separate random profile IDs. Joining requires community-rule agreement. Discovery defaults on during explicit signup; online visibility defaults off.
- Online means activity within Social in the last 90 seconds. The UI heartbeats every 30 seconds while foregrounded, refreshes directory/connections every 15 seconds, and polls the open conversation every 4 seconds. This is bounded polling, not WebSocket presence or push notifications.
- A recipient must accept a pending follow before either party can message. Pair locks serialize connection changes, blocks, reads and sends. Blocking removes the connection; unblocking never restores consent. Removing a connection closes access to chat history until another request is accepted.
- Blocked members' forum content is hidden in both directions. Posts are member-visible, even when a profile is hidden from People. Deleting a topic closes the entire discussion. Messages, topics and replies have stable request IDs for safe retries.
- Reports include the selected content snapshot. Only the recipient can report a private message, and moderators see only the reported snapshot. Moderator actions are recorded with actor, decision and timestamp. Moderator assignment cannot come from client metadata.
- Limits are durable and transactional: 60 messages/minute, 20 follow requests/hour, 10 topics/hour, 60 replies/hour, 20 reports/day. Lists have bounded pages; messages/replies use sequence cursors. Directory/topics use offset pagination and literal partial search.

## Moderator setup

No account is automatically made a moderator. The project owner must explicitly assign a **verified KORLIX account** using the Supabase SQL editor, then that account joins Social and reopens it to see **Social settings → Moderation reports**:

```sql
-- Replace the placeholder only with the account selected by the owner.
insert into public.korlix_social_moderators(user_id)
select id from auth.users
where lower(email) = lower('SELECTED_MODERATOR_EMAIL')
  and email_confirmed_at is not null
on conflict do nothing;
```

Before broad community promotion, assign a moderator and establish a report review process. There is no automatic report adjudication. Suspension restoration and unlocking are owner operations in the database in v1. Ordinary users can edit/remove their own content and unblock members in the UI.

## Profiles, photos and calling (September 28 update)

- Profession is optional (100 characters), member-visible, and included in partial People/connection searches. Older clients that omit it preserve the saved value.
- A separate authenticated multipart route accepts one still JPG/PNG/WebP up to 8 MB and 32 megapixels. Sharp applies EXIF rotation, center-crops to 512×512, re-encodes JPEG and removes metadata. Profile creation precedes uploads. Private bucket `korlix-social-avatars` has a restrictive browser-deny policy; the server signs only authorized member-card photo paths for ten minutes. Replacements/removal delete the previous object. Signed URLs are bearer links until they expire or the object is removed; downloaded photos cannot be recalled.
- Emoji remain ordinary Unicode message text. The client includes a searchable picker and cursor-aware insertion; the API decodes UTF-8 explicitly.
- Audio/video calls use Flutter WebRTC. Caller and recipient need Social open in the foreground; incoming invitations poll every three seconds. The recipient must tap Answer before devices open. A session-specific random device ID claims the call, preventing another tab from taking over. Calls between nonconnections, blocked or suspended members are rejected.
- Signaling uses service-only `korlix_social_calls_v1`, with two RLS-protected tables. No audio/video recordings are made or stored. Offer/answer roles and ownership are enforced; signaling retries use stable UUIDs, signals use sequence cursors, and crossed/busy calls serialize under ordered participant locks. Network candidates can expose each caller's network address to the other participant; this is direct WebRTC, not an anonymity feature.
- Ringing expires after 45 seconds, stale participants after 60 seconds, and calls after four hours. Foreground clients poll every two seconds and stop devices on logout, route disposal, hidden/paused app state, rejected access, permission failure or prolonged network loss. Blocks/removal are rechecked on every signaling/poll action. Signals are deleted at call termination; old call rows and their signals are deleted opportunistically when a participant next checks their inbox after 24 hours. This is not a scheduled retention guarantee.
- Ten new calls per ten minutes per account; 300 signaling writes/minute, bounded payloads and pages. `SOCIAL_CALLS_ENABLED=false` disables starting/answering calls without affecting text chat or ending calls.

### Relay configuration

Direct calling uses Google's public STUN endpoints. No new provider subscription is created. Some mobile, corporate and symmetric-NAT networks require TURN. Set optional backend environment variable `SOCIAL_ICE_SERVERS` to a JSON array of WebRTC ICE servers, e.g. `[{"urls":["turns:YOUR_RELAY:443"],"username":"ISSUED_USERNAME","credential":"ISSUED_CREDENTIAL"}]`. Use credentials intended for client-side TURN access and rotate them; never use a provider management API key. The authenticated call configuration endpoint supplies ICE credentials to the calling client. Do not claim universal connectivity until a relay is configured and tested on the target networks.

## Replies to specific chat messages

Apply `20260928225119_korlix_social_message_replies.sql` before deploying the reply-aware backend and frontend. `send` accepts optional `reply_to`; `messages` includes a bounded original-message preview; `GET message` opens one original message within the same accepted conversation. All three use the service-only, security-invoker `korlix_social_chat_v1` RPC. Existing plain-message clients remain compatible.

The server checks both participants, current connection/block state, and the original message’s conversation. Reply previews resolve from the original row rather than preserving a text snapshot, so removing an original hides its text in subsequent responses. Retries must keep the same body and reply target to reuse the same message ID. ## Group chats

Apply `20260928231631_korlix_social_groups.sql` before deploying the group-aware backend, then deploy the frontend. **Social → Groups → Create a group** supports a group name and selecting multiple accepted connections. Invitations appear in the recipient's Groups tab; no external messages, email invitations, or push notifications are sent. Groups hold up to 50 people including pending invitations. The owner can invite, rename, remove members, or cancel invitations. Members can leave; ownership passes to the oldest joined active member, or the group closes if none remain.

The `korlix_social_groups_v1` service-only security-invoker RPC checks current membership on every request. Group locks serialize membership and message operations. Pending, declined, removed, and former members cannot read or send messages. Joining/rejoining sets a sequence boundary: only messages sent after that join are available. Earlier messages and their quoted previews remain hidden. Creating a group and its batch of invitations is atomic; stable group/message UUIDs make identical retries safe. Creation retries compare the original name and normalized recipient set. Invites require an accepted connection to the current owner, and admission checks blocks against active group members.

Three new tables have RLS, explicit browser-role denial, and indexes for membership, timeline and foreign-key access. Group cards expose no auth identity or email address. Blocking hides the blocked sender's group text and quotes in either direction; visible clients redact previously loaded content on the next successful refresh. Owners' member controls and all writes are checked again on the server. Quoted replies, Unicode/emoji, message removal, unread counts and sequence pagination work in group chats. Group calling and chat file attachments are not part of this release.

Limits: 10 group creations/day, 100 new member invitations/day, 60 group messages/minute, 20 reports/day shared with other Social reports. Retired invitations require one hour before inviting the same person again. Group reports contain only the selected authorized message snapshot and enter the existing moderator queue; removal is supported by the group RPC while other reports retain the original moderation behavior. Report resolution records the verified moderator and decision.

Tests use local PGlite/HTTP and Flutter fixtures, covering multi-select/search, acceptance, retries, role restrictions, revocation, ownership transfer, history boundaries, quotes, blocked content, reports, paging, unread counts, capacity, mobile layouts and account changes. No production users are invited or contacted by validation.

## Profile albums (October 2 update)

Apply `20261002033000_korlix_social_albums.sql` before the backend. Social profiles now have named photo albums, multi-select upload (20 at once), covers, photo/album deletion and a swipe/zoom viewer. Each profile holds 20 albums, 100 photos per album, 500 photos overall. Images are capped at 8 MB and 32 megapixels, re-encoded as JPEG within 2048×2048 without cropping, with EXIF/location metadata removed. HEIC works only where the device picker or image decoder supports conversion; otherwise the UI requests JPG/PNG/WebP.

Albums default to accepted connections. Owners can choose Only me or Social members. Hidden profiles remain unavailable to nonconnections; blocks/suspensions apply in both directions. All tables and RPCs remain service-only with RLS. Private `korlix-social-albums` storage denies browser roles even when unrelated permissive policies exist. Only authorized reads receive five-minute signed links; links already issued and photos already saved cannot be recalled. Removal deletes storage objects after tombstoning the rows, with idempotent retries. A network failure after a database commit preserves the candidate storage object to avoid deleting a successfully saved photo. Uncommitted objects from ambiguous transport failures may need later cleanup. No production member photos are used in automated tests.

The frontend persists each upload's request ID for retry, reports partial success, retries only failures, and clears displayed photos on account changes. Web delivery is through the frontend Render deployment; installed mobile clients need a new store build.

## Calling recovery (October 2 update)

WebRTC returns fresh Dart wrappers for the same underlying microphone track. Recovery now matches track IDs, so Reconnect microphone replaces the actual audio sender. A blocked audio playback promise cannot stall video-track attachment. Native video calls default to speaker output. Device errors distinguish denied permission, a busy capture device and missing hardware.

`call_config` still prefers `SOCIAL_ICE_SERVERS`. If no authenticated TURN relay is configured, existing `TWILIO_ACCOUNT_SID` plus `TWILIO_AUTH_TOKEN` (or `TWILIO_API_KEY` + `TWILIO_API_SECRET`) can issue short-lived TURN credentials. Management credentials never enter client responses or logs. `SOCIAL_TURN_PROVIDER=disabled` disables this fallback; `SOCIAL_CALLS_ENABLED=false` remains the call kill switch. Tokens last four hours to match the call lifetime, cache for five minutes per authenticated member, and use Twilio's existing account/relay usage billing. No subscription or new service is created. Startup logs report only `configured`, `twilio`, or `direct-only`; configured readiness is not proof of a successful relay allocation. Failed provider requests return explicit `relayStatus: unavailable` and preserve direct calling.

Required live check: two accounts/devices must exchange audible speech and video, including Wi-Fi ↔ mobile data. Local mocks or same-browser WebRTC tests cannot establish Safari/iPad permissions or cross-network TURN operation. Browser integration test: run `flutter test --platform chrome test/social_call_browser_test.dart` with Chrome configured for synthetic media; this test never calls real users.

## Public walls and optional profile details (October 3 update)

Social opens on **Wall**, with **Following**, **Explore**, and **My wall** feeds. Following includes the member's own posts and posts from accepted connections. Existing follow approval still applies. Public means visible to signed-in KORLIX Social members, subject to blocks, suspension, and moderation. Wall posts reuse the discussion, reply, editing, removal, reporting, and rate-limit controls. They remain separate from category forums. A headline is optional; text is required. Member cards and wall authors open a profile with Wall and About views, plus existing connection, messaging, and album actions.

Profiles support an optional status caption, home country, city, phone number, favorite color, profession, current job, favorite food, marital status, and income level. Each field has its own **Public**, **Followers**, or **Only me** audience. Phone number and income level default to Only me. Followers means an accepted Social connection. Blank values clear the field; clients that omit new keys preserve existing values and audience settings. Values are voluntary free text and never populated from account email, billing, location, or inferred personal data. Income can include a range and currency chosen by the member. Status captions stay on the profile until edited or cleared.

Visibility is enforced before the API returns data. A conservative legacy member card omits new details; viewer-aware profile and wall responses include only permitted values. Private profession values cannot be discovered through People or connection searches. Auth user IDs, emails, and the owner's audience map are not exposed to other members. Blocking and connection removal also clear the relevant cached views immediately. The main feed refreshes while Social is foregrounded; a profile can be refreshed manually.

Apply `20261003151739_korlix_social_wall_profiles.sql` before the backend and frontend. Schema additions preserve current profiles, forums, conversations, groups, calls, and albums. The additive migration preserves current clients and uses the existing private attachment bucket. Voice-only replies and empty captions on existing voice replies are supported; text-only replies still require text. Recordings use validated mono 16-bit PCM WAV, 300 ms to 3 minutes, under the existing 20 MB upload and 250 MB member storage limits.

Attachments are bound to their owner and exact wall post. Forums, direct messages, and groups cannot reuse a wall draft. Publishing requires an active member, available unlocked wall post, ready unexpired draft, and exact retry identity. Existing connection, block, suspension, reporting, deletion, and rate limits remain enforced. Reports identify voice-only content and preserve safe audio metadata without storage paths or signed links.

Wall reply cards include attachment metadata with `scope: wall`, but no cached playback URL. `GET /api/social/attachment_link?id=<attachment UUID>` checks current post/reply visibility and signs a 60-second URL on demand. Blocking or removal immediately denies new playback links; an already issued bearer URL can remain valid until expiry, and downloaded/buffered audio cannot be recalled. Soft deletion, moderation removal, and hard deletion queue storage cleanup; deleting a post also removes its unused drafts. The cleanup worker deletes storage objects before acknowledging metadata rows. All new functions remain security invokers restricted to `service_role`; browser roles have no direct table, RPC, or bucket access.

## Auto Dump selected messages (October 3 update)

Apply `20261003183004_korlix_social_auto_dump.sql` before deploying the backend and frontend. A member can remove one sent or received direct/group message from **their own history** after 15 seconds, 45 seconds, 1 minute, 15 minutes, 1 hour or 1 day. Shared rows and attachments remain intact for other participants; this is not recipient recall or secure destruction. Existing signed URLs can remain valid until their original expiry, and downloads/screenshots cannot be recalled.

`POST /api/social/dump_schedule` accepts `id`, exactly one `peer` or `group`, `seconds` from `[15,45,60,900,3600,86400]`, and a stable UUID `request_id`. `POST /api/social/dump_cancel` accepts the same destination and IDs without `seconds`. Reuse the operation's request ID after uncertain network failures; use a new ID for a deliberate change. Both return `{id,dump_at,server_time,dumped}`. An old request replay returns the effective current state without replaying old intent. Cancellation/rescheduling is allowed only before expiry; an expired message returns `dumped:true` and cannot be restored. The server derives the actor exclusively from the verified session.

History and original-message responses include `server_time`; message and nested quote cards include viewer-only `dump_at`. History returns authoritative `dump_schedules` (all future deadlines keyed by message UUID) and `dumped_ids` (all expired IDs) for that conversation, including earlier pages. Clients reconcile cached pages against both and account for device clock skew. SQL applies expiry before pagination and to unread counts, previews, quoted originals, reply targets, attachment links, and attached-upload retries. The timestamp is checked on every read, so expiry works with the app closed and needs no cron job. A per-actor transaction lock, real server clock and immutable request ledger prevent retries or late cancellation from resurrecting messages. New tables have browser-deny RLS and service-only invoker functions. The migration stops if any replaced RPC differs from its reviewed baseline.

## Communication upgrade (October 4 update)

Calls now retain a private 30-day recent-call history, including incoming, outgoing, missed and declined calls. SDP and ICE signals are removed immediately when a call ends. History never exposes device IDs, signal payloads, or another account's call rows. Blocked/suspended peers are redacted and cannot be called back. History and inbox reads resolve timed-out calls; the notification worker also expires stale calls when browser push is configured.

### Opt-in browser notifications

The backend uses standards-based Web Push and an application VAPID key pair. Configure the existing backend service with `SOCIAL_WEB_PUSH_PUBLIC_KEY`, `SOCIAL_WEB_PUSH_PRIVATE_KEY`, and `SOCIAL_WEB_PUSH_SUBJECT` (the operator's HTTPS website or `mailto:` contact). Generate the pair once using the pinned `web-push` package; preserve the private key as a server secret. `SOCIAL_WEB_PUSH_ENABLED=false` stops delivery. Configuration readiness confirms a matching local key pair, not a successful device delivery. A VAPID rotation requires browser subscriptions to be renewed. No Google Firebase billing product or new service is created by this integration.

Verified Social members explicitly enable calls and/or messages for each browser. The API accepts at most ten devices per member and only vendor HTTPS push endpoints with validated browser encryption keys. `push_state` exposes preferences without endpoint secrets; `push_unsubscribe` deletes only the current member's chosen device and its queued work. Subscriptions cannot be reassigned between accounts. The browser clears its binding, closes notifications and unsubscribes on logout/account switches; a fresh subscription is needed for a different account. Subscriptions expire after ninety days without renewal.

Database triggers enqueue only new calls/direct messages/group messages for already subscribed browsers. There is no historical backfill. Notification text is generic: no sender name, message contents, attachment links, or call/network details. The payload includes a random browser binding, opaque event ID, fixed Social URL, and expiry. The service worker checks its current binding before showing anything and drops expired call invitations. Opening a notification still requires the signed-in app to load authorized data; answering always requires a user action.

A five-second worker claims durable, leased rows. Calls take priority and expire after 45 seconds; unread message alerts expire after five minutes. Unsent message bursts from the same sender coalesce, and a recipient's pending message queue is bounded by a 500-row admission threshold (up to ten browser rows per event) without rejecting ordinary chat writes. Immediately before each send, the database rechecks current subscription/binding, accepted connections or group membership, suspension, blocks, unread state, deletion and viewer Auto Dump expiry. Permission changes cancel unsent work; already accepted push cannot be recalled, and the operating system ultimately controls delivery/display. Known transient provider rejections receive up to three attempts; uncertain network results and interrupted sending leases are not automatically replayed. Expired browser subscriptions are removed after provider 404/410 responses. Outbox metadata expires after seven days.

Only the public VAPID key reaches the frontend. Subscription encryption keys and endpoints stay in service-only RLS tables. Worker/user RPC actions are separate, all functions are security invokers, and direct browser table/RPC execution is denied. Web Push requests use a fixed browser-vendor hostname allowlist, never follow redirects, and have an eight-second socket timeout. Tests inject a provider fixture and never contact real endpoints.

Browser background notifications require browser/OS permission and support. iPhone/iPad web push requires an eligible Home Screen web app; delivery and an ongoing background microphone/camera session remain subject to OS/browser limits. **Native FCM/APNs and OS incoming-call screens are not enabled by this web release.** They require platform credentials, an updated signed store build, platform call lifecycle integration and real-device testing. A frontend screen change within the active app can preserve a call, but this does not guarantee media while the app is suspended or closed.
